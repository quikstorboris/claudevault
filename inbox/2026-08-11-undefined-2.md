# 

Swept the entire C:\KoBre Dropbox\QS Fileserver\Shared\QMS Onboarding tree (247 client folders) for lease/rental/agreement/contract documents to build a real-world corpus for the QMS Template Tagging Assistant, per Boris's request while he was away for the evening. Findings:

SCALE: 662 candidate files total (452 .docx, 155 .pdf, 55 .doc) across 247 client folders. Of the 452 .docx: 258 already contain {{tag}} syntax (already-tagged production templates), 194 are genuinely raw/untagged.

BIGGEST FINDING -- a real tag-usage harvest: extracted every {{tag_key}} occurrence from all 258 already-tagged documents (cheap, in-memory zip-entry scan, no per-file extraction needed) and got 125 distinct real tag keys with production usage frequency, saved to all_tags_found.csv. Key discoveries: (1) e.name (combined full name, 479 uses) is used vastly more than e.fname/e.lname split (~8-9 uses each) -- e.name should likely be the default/primary name tag going forward, not the fname/lname split originally assumed. (2) e.a.* (alternate contact) confirmed as a full family: name, add1/add2, city, state, post, phone, email, zip, rel (relationship), fname/lname (rare). (3) e.m.* (military) expanded well beyond what was known: branch, cophone, colname/cofname, id, eserv/sserv (likely service start/end), unit (tenant's military unit, not storage unit), a.fname/a.lname (agent sub-family). (4) f.* (facility) confirmed with full breakdown including f.ow.firstname/f.ow.lastname (facility owner name, split). (5) m.opi.* is a lienholder-info family used far more often (27/23/18/12 occurrences) than the m.vi.lhfn/lha/lhpn variant found earlier in bluebird (2 occurrences each) -- opi is the dominant real-world convention, not vi.lh*. (6) e.keycodes (15 uses) resolves the previously-flagged "no access/gate code tag confirmed" gap. (7) e.comp (20 uses) likely resolves the "business/company tenant name" gap flagged repeatedly across independent documents (Affordable Storage, All-in-One, South Daytona, TSSA's official form all separately ask for a tenant's business name if applicable). (8) e.init (34 uses) suggests the scattered acknowledgment-initial blanks seen across many documents DO have a real tag, contradicting the earlier assumption that these are categorically out-of-scope -- worth re-examining. (9) m.ptd+1/l.ptd+1 (37/2 uses) confirms the {{tag+N}} date-offset arithmetic syntax already flagged as an open question in the vault, now with real frequency data proving it's commonly used, not a one-off. (10) Vehicle family confirmed under BOTH m.vi.* and l.vi.* prefixes (the same m.*/l.* duality pattern applies to vehicle info, not just lease dates). (11) Real production typos found: add1/add2 (missing prefix), e.a..name (double dot), f.add.2 (extra dot), a.add1 (missing e. prefix) -- concrete evidence of tag-integrity drift at scale, same bug class as the previously-found Courtesy Notice and Bluebird missing-brace bugs.

RAW DOCUMENT SAMPLE FINDINGS (5 additional documents deep-read: N&R Properties RV/mini storage, RV Repair Specialist, South Daytona vehicle/vessel lease, the official Texas Self Storage Association standardized form, Pad-N-Loc consolidated rules): (1) The TSSA official form is a standardized industry template likely reused verbatim or near-verbatim across many independent Texas facilities -- worth prioritizing since supporting it well could generalize across many real clients at once, unlike one-off custom templates. (2) New vehicle/vessel fields beyond what Bluebird showed: Hull ID No., Vessel Name, Trailer Description/Tag. (3) Checkboxes are pervasive across the whole corpus, not a one-off -- 77 of 194 raw documents (40%) mention military/armed-forces status specifically, and the TSSA form alone has 5+ separate checkboxes (individual-vs-business, military, reserves-vs-active-duty, multiple addendum-attached checkboxes). Already deferred/vaulted per Boris's instruction, but scale confirmed. (4) Two new possible gaps: "Employer's name" (TSSA) and "Applicable Sales Tax" (South Daytona) -- no corresponding tags found anywhere in the 125-tag harvest.
